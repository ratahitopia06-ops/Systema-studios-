The initial thesis

“The fact that I am experiencing existence now establishes that subjective existence is possible. It does not by itself establish that this particular observer will exist again. The central problem therefore becomes whether first-person identity requires continuous existence, or whether consciousness can meaningfully recur across an interval containing no experience.”

What it investigates

Logical coherence, assumptions, identity criteria, first-person time, possible recurrence, and the difference between conceptual models and physical claims.

What it will not do

It will not certify reincarnation, communication across dimensions, or an extraordinary explanation merely because an experience feels compelling.

Evidence discipline

Subjective experience, objective evidence, speculation, and established science remain distinct throughout each investigation.

Response standard

Every substantial claim is examined through the same lens.

  1. Claim and formal argument
  2. Validity, soundness, and hidden assumptions
  3. Strongest objection and strongest defence
  4. Scientific and philosophical status
  5. What remains open or currently unknowable

Important: This is a philosophical reasoning tool, not a source of clinical, spiritual, or scientific certification. If an experience feels frightening, urgent, or destabilising, seek support from someone you trust or a qualified local professional.
